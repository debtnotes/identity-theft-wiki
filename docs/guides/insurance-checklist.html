Insurance checklist: documents and questions before purchase

Request these documents first

Item Why it matters
Exact plan name and state-specific certificate/policy Marketing pages often omit terms that control coverage.
Policy effective date and insurer/underwriter Identifies the legal contract and regulator context.
Full exclusions and definitions Determines whether a loss is an identity-theft event at all.
Schedule of benefits and sublimits A headline limit may include smaller caps for wages, legal help, or cash recovery.
Claim, notice, proof, and consent rules Late notice or unapproved expenses can matter.
Other-insurance clause Existing home, card, or employer benefits may pay first.
Renewal, cancellation, and refund terms Separates introductory price from ongoing cost and exit friction.

Ask the provider these questions in writing

  1. Does restoration assistance apply to an event first suspected before enrollment? Does insurance reimbursement apply separately?
  2. Is the event date based on occurrence, discovery, report, or membership start? Define each term.
  3. Are cash-loss benefits inside the aggregate limit or in addition to it?
  4. Must the bank, card issuer, employer plan, or homeowners insurer be pursued first?
  5. What does the provider mean by “restoration”? Can it contact a creditor or collector on the member’s written authority?
  6. What deadline applies to notice, a police/FTC report, and proof of loss?
  7. Do legal, accounting, or investigator expenses need prior written approval?
  8. What terms apply in the consumer’s state, and can the certificate be sent before enrollment?

Red flags

  • “Up to $1M” is presented without a certificate or benefit schedule.
  • The representative will not state how a pre-enrollment incident is treated.
  • The policy says a complete version is available on request, but it is not supplied before purchase.
  • The plan combines monitoring and insurance claims without identifying which service covers which event.
