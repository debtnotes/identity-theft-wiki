# If a debt collector contacts you

Do not ignore a collection contact, but do not share sensitive financial
information before confirming the collector and debt are legitimate.

- Ask for the collector’s name, company, address, and phone number.
- Request the validation information in writing. CFPB says it generally
  identifies the creditor, account, amount, and how to dispute the debt.
- If the debt is not yours, the written-dispute timing matters: CFPB explains
  that a written dispute within the stated 30-day period generally requires the
  collector to pause collection until it provides verification.
- Preserve the validation notice, communications, and proof of any dispute.
- If the item is also on a credit report, consider the separate credit-report
  dispute and identity-theft-blocking processes.

Deadlines, lawsuits, and state-law issues can be consequential. Consider a
qualified consumer-law attorney if a collector threatens litigation, continues
collection after a timely written dispute, or the matter cannot be resolved.

Read the CFPB’s [validation-notice guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-information-does-a-debt-collector-have-to-give-me-about-the-debt-en-331/) and [debt-collection overview](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/debt-collection/know-your-rights-when-a-debt-collector-calls/).
