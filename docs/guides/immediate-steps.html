Immediate steps for suspicious identity activity

Use this as a general starting point when an unfamiliar account, charge, notice, or debt appears. It does not establish that identity theft occurred.

  1. Contact the affected company. Use a known phone number, website, or app; ask for its fraud process and preserve the confirmation.
  2. Secure affected accounts. Change passwords and PINs through trusted channels. Do not use links from unsolicited messages.
  3. Review all three credit reports. Use AnnualCreditReport.com, the official request site. The FTC says reports from each nationwide bureau are available weekly. An item missing from a credit report does not prove that no misuse occurred, because not all creditors furnish data.
  4. Consider a security freeze or fraud alert. These protections work differently; see Credit freezes and fraud alerts.
  5. Create a recovery plan. IdentityTheft.gov, operated by the FTC, can create an FTC Identity Theft Report and personal recovery plan.
  6. Keep records. Save reports, letters, notices, screenshots, mailing receipts, dates, and names of contacts. Do not put unredacted identifiers in an online note or email.

This sequence is drawn from FTC recovery guidance and IdentityTheft.gov. See sources for access dates and direct links.
