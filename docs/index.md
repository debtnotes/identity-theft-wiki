# Identity Fraud Knowledge Base

Practical, evidence-first public guidance for responding to possible identity
fraud in the United States.

> **Important:** This site provides general information, not legal, tax,
> insurance, or financial advice. If there is an unfamiliar account, charge, or
> collection attempt, act promptly and preserve records.

## Start here

1. Follow the [immediate steps](guides/immediate-steps.md) for suspicious
   activity.
2. Learn when a [security freeze or fraud alert](guides/freezes-and-alerts.md)
   makes sense.
3. If a collector contacts you, read [how to respond safely](guides/debt-collectors.md).

## What this site does—and does not—claim

The initial material is based on official FTC, CFPB, IRS, and statutory sources
accessed on August 18, 2026. It does not diagnose a particular situation or
endorse an identity-protection provider. Paid monitoring, restoration, and
insurance are being researched separately, using controlling provider policy
documents and state-specific terms where applicable.

Read the [research method](research/scope-and-method.md) and [source register](research/sources.md).
