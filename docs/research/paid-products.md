# Paid monitoring, restoration, and insurance

## Research status: no recommendation yet

Paid identity products are not interchangeable:

- **Monitoring** usually alerts after a monitored record changes.
- **Restoration** may provide human help with recovery tasks.
- **Insurance** may reimburse specified recovery costs, subject to its terms.

The CFPB cautions that monitoring does not prevent identity theft. Free credit
reports and security freezes can cover important detection and prevention needs.
See the CFPB’s [credit-monitoring overview](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-monitoring-service-en-1365/).

Before any provider is described as a fit, this project will review its
controlling policy/certificate and answer, at minimum:

1. Is a known or pre-enrollment incident excluded?
2. Does restoration differ from insurance eligibility?
3. Which expenses, limits, deductibles, approval rules, and deadlines apply?
4. Are the terms different in the consumer’s state?
5. What does the service actually monitor beyond the three credit files?

An advertised "$1 million" or "$3 million" limit is not a coverage conclusion.

## First-pass document discovery

The research repository has located first-pass official product pages and/or
plan-specific summaries for Experian IdentityWorks, Aura, LifeLock, and Identity
Guard. Several of those sources themselves direct readers to a master policy,
certificate, or full summary for the terms that control. The project is therefore
not publishing a comparative ranking, price table, or coverage claim yet.

The next review stage is to collect the exact plan and state-specific documents,
then test their definitions, exclusions, notice rules, reimbursement sublimits,
and—most importantly—treatment of a suspicious event known before enrollment.
