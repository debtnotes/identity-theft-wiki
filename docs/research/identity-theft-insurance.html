Identity-theft insurance encyclopedia

What it is—and is not

Identity-theft insurance commonly reimburses certain eligible expenses of recovering from identity fraud. It may address legal costs, lost wages, notarization/postage, document costs, and some unreimbursed electronic-fund transfer loss. It is not a blanket guarantee against fraud and does not insure a person’s net worth, credit score, home equity, or retirement balance.

The NAIC notes that identity-theft coverage may be built into or added to homeowners coverage, or purchased separately. The exact policy governs.

Coverage anatomy

Trigger and timing

Most important: determine whether the event must occur or be first discovered while an active member. Experian’s located Plus summary, for example, says it covers a stolen-identity event only if first discovered during membership and reported within its stated period. Aura’s located $1M summary requires a membership-term occurrence and uses a discovery-based occurrence-date definition. Those summaries are plan-specific and the full policy controls.

Aggregate limits and sublimits

An aggregate is the maximum total the insurer will pay under the stated scope. It may be per person, family, policy period, or occurrence. A cash-recovery benefit can sit inside that total; it is not automatically additional money. Check wage, legal-consultation, childcare, travel, and expert-cost sublimits.

Other insurance and institutional recovery

Some plans require or assume that a financial institution, card issuer, or other coverage pays first. The located Experian Plus summary expressly describes its coverage as excess over other applicable insurance. A buyer should preserve bank claim records and ask how the policy coordinates with an employer benefit or home-policy endorsement.

Claim duties

Common requirements include prompt notice, reports to affected institutions, proof of loss, preserving receipts, and insurer consent before retaining an attorney or investigator. These are not technicalities: the policy conditions are part of the coverage decision.

Short policy-language example: Aura’s 2025 summary labels its cash-recovery aggregate “part of and not in addition to” expense reimbursement. Read the current certificate for the purchaser’s plan and state.

What an excellent-credit, high-asset household should focus on

A low-800s score indicates strong existing credit history; it does not establish that fraud is impossible or that a credit-monitoring plan will protect accounts. The practical priorities are account security, three-bureau freezes, direct institutional alerts, adequate liability/property coverage, and documented recovery procedures. Paid identity coverage is a supplemental decision after those controls and existing benefits are reviewed.
